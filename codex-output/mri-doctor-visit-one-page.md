# MRI Doctor Visit Summary

**Purpose:** Concise discussion aid based on the wording of the MRI report. This is not a diagnosis.

## Top 3 MRI Findings in Plain English

1. **L4-L5 - most important reported level:** The report describes a disc bulge/protrusion and thickened nearby tissues causing **moderate-to-severe narrowing** of the central nerve passage, with pressure on traversing nerve roots and crowding of the cauda equina nerve fibers.
2. **L5-S1:** A bulging disc with an annular tear and thickened ligament causes **mild central narrowing**, with reported contact on the right traversing nerve root and compression effect on the left traversing nerve root.
3. **L3-L4:** A broad disc bulge and thickened ligament cause **mild narrowing** of the central canal and nerve exit spaces, touching traversing nerve roots on both sides.

## Symptoms to Tell the Doctor Clearly

- Lower-back pain or stiffness: location, severity, and what triggers or relieves it.
- Pain, tingling, or numbness into the buttock, hip area, thigh, leg, or foot; note the side and exact path.
- Any leg or foot weakness, tripping, balance trouble, or difficulty with stairs.
- Reduced ability to stand or walk: how far/how long, and whether sitting or bending forward helps.
- Any bladder, bowel, sexual-function, or groin/saddle-area sensation changes, including when they began.
- How symptoms have changed over time and how they affect daily activities or sleep.

## Questions for the Spine Specialist

1. How significant is the `L4-L5` narrowing and nerve crowding when matched to my symptoms and examination?
2. Which reported nerve-root findings best match any leg, foot, or walking symptoms I describe?
3. Do these two report entries represent different scans, and is there evidence of change over time?
4. Should the original MRI images be reviewed directly to confirm the reported nerve compression?
5. Which new or worsening symptoms should prompt urgent or emergency assessment?

## Urgent Red Flags

Seek prompt emergency medical assessment for **new bladder or bowel control problems**, difficulty starting or sensing urination, **new numbness in the saddle/groin area**, **rapidly worsening leg weakness**, or severe progressively worsening neurological symptoms.

## Important Note

An MRI report shows structural findings, but it cannot by itself establish the cause of symptoms. These findings must be matched with the symptom history, neurological examination, and review by the treating clinician.
